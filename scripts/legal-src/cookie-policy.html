What cookies actually are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store. On your next visit the browser sends it back, which is how a site can recognise a returning visitor or remember a preference. Cookies cannot execute programs, cannot read other files on your device, and cannot install software.

Related technologies do similar jobs by different means, and where we mention cookies below we include these:

  • Local storage — data stored by the browser without being sent on every request. We use it for interface preferences.
  • Pixels and tags — a tiny image or script whose loading records that a page was viewed.
  • Session identifiers — a short-lived token that keeps you signed in while you move between administrative pages.

How to control cookies

In your browser

Every major browser lets you block or delete cookies, and block third-party cookies specifically. Blocking all cookies will break sign-in on many sites, including our administrative area, but will not prevent you reading anything on this site.

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy and security → Third-party cookies
  • Firefox: Settings → Privacy & Security → Cookies and Site Data
  • Safari: Settings → Privacy → Prevent cross-site tracking
  • Edge: Settings → Cookies and site permissions
  • Android / iOS browsers: the equivalent settings under Privacy

Targeted opt-outs

  • Google Analytics: the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
  • Everything at once: private or incognito browsing discards cookies when you close the window.

Browsers also send a "Do Not Track" or Global Privacy Control signal if you enable it. Support for these signals across the industry is inconsistent; our own first-party analytics respects them, but we cannot guarantee the behaviour of third-party networks.
