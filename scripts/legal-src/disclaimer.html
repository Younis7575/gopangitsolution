How to read our cost figures

Several of our guides publish rupee and dollar ranges for building software. We are more specific than most agencies are willing to be, so it is worth being equally specific about the limits of those numbers.

Where the figures come from. They are derived from our own quoted and delivered projects, plus rates we observe in the local market. They are not survey data, they are not industry benchmarks, and they are not audited.

What they assume. Ranges assume a conventional scope, an engaged client, reasonably clean input data, and third-party systems that behave as their documentation says. Any of those assumptions failing moves the number, usually upward.

Why your quote may differ legitimately. A quote from us or from anyone else may fall well outside a published range because of scope detail we could not anticipate, integration complexity, compliance requirements, timeline compression, currency movement since publication, or the seniority of the team assigned. A difference is not evidence that a quote is unfair.

Currency and time. Figures are stated in the currency shown, at the time of writing. Exchange rates, local wage levels, cloud pricing and third-party subscription costs all change. An article's publication date is shown on the page; treat older figures with proportionate caution.

The short rule

Use our published ranges to sanity-check a budget or to spot a quote that is wildly out of line. Do not use them as a negotiating position against a specific vendor, and do not treat them as an offer from us. Only a written quote for your project, referencing a dated scope, is an offer.

Technical opinions and comparisons

Our comparison articles — frameworks, backends, hosting tiers, e-commerce platforms — contain our professional opinion as of the date of publication. They are honest and they are also partial: they reflect the kinds of projects we take on and the technologies our team knows well.

Software moves quickly. A framework limitation we describe may be resolved in a later release; a package we call mature may become unmaintained; a provider's pricing or regional availability may change. Verify current facts — especially version support, pricing, and whether a service is available in your country — against the vendor's own documentation before relying on them.

Where we name third-party products, we are describing them, not certifying them. We have no affiliate arrangements, sponsorships or paid placements with any product mentioned on this site. If that ever changes, we will disclose it on the page concerned.
